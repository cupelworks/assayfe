How to read the charts

Each chart shows a result next to the line it's judged against.

Drawn here by the same charts a batch's and a comparison's pages use. Each part is numbered; beside each chart, how a verdict reads off it.

Red is always the line a result is judged against: the target, the check's threshold, or 0 for no change.

Reading it
The whole range right of the line: Passed.
The line inside the range: Inconclusive - the row says how large a new batch would likely settle it, or that it would take more than a batch can run.
The whole range left of the line: Failed.

Every row of a batch shares the axis; it starts at 50% unless a range reaches lower. 29 of 29 is just enough at 90% - 28 of 28 isn't: its range starts at 89.9%.

Grey: no value - the check couldn't run (no judge chosen, say), or the run was Not Ran. It counts neither way: the sample is the green and red squares. A check with no green or red square gets no verdict; when that's every check, the batch is Not Ran.

On a batch's page each square opens its run, to see what was answered then.

03

The runs grid

At the top of a batch's page: a row per entry (or the one test), a square per time.

Each square is one run's own status: Green when every check was met in it, Amber when some were, Red when none was, Not Ran when nothing could be evaluated.

While the batch runs, the grid fills in from Pending to Running to an outcome. It shows statuses only - never a verdict, which waits until every run has finished.

A column of Amber across every entry points at one bad time - an outage, say; a row of it, at one entry.

04

The met rate over time

Open a check on a batch's page. The same 26 of 28 as above.

The line is the met rate after each time: one miss early on swings it a lot (time 6: from 100% to 83%), the same miss late barely moves it (time 21).

An early streak can be luck. The verdict uses every time, which is why a running batch shows no verdict: stopping after a good start could turn luck into a pass.

Reading it
The whole range on the passing side of the line: Passed.
The line inside the range: Inconclusive.
The whole range on the other side: Failed.
Too few scores to decide: No verdict - the row says why.
